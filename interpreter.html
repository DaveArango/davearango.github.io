01 · Professional journey

From curiosity to a vocation.

My path as a Colombian Sign Language (LSC) interpreter and translator did not begin with a fixed plan. It began with curiosity: a desire to understand a world that I had previously seen through a much narrower perspective.

01
Learning through people

Every stage has connected language, community, professional practice and a constant need to keep learning.

The beginning

Curiosity became a direction.

My first foundations in interpreting were born from curiosity. I joined my first Colombian Sign Language courses simply because I wanted to learn. Those spaces gradually changed the way I understood communication, society and other people's experiences.

I began developing new skills and social understandings that eventually became part of who I am. More importantly, I met people who influenced both my personal development and my professional vocation. That experience created a strong desire to contribute in some meaningful way.

Early practice

Learning by doing.

I continued taking different courses and started taking my first steps as an interpreter. At that time, I was still learning the concepts, methods, modalities and techniques behind LSC–Spanish interpreting. I did not have all the answers, but the lack of knowledge became a reason to investigate rather than a reason to stop.

My curiosity and passion for knowledge pushed me to explore the theory and practice surrounding LSC–Spanish interpreting more deeply.

A path shaped by learning

Milestones that changed my perspective.

First courses

Discovering LSC and the Deaf community

What began as curiosity developed into relationships, communication skills and a growing awareness of the cultural and social dimensions of language.

Professional formation

Taking interpreting more seriously

I was encouraged to train as an interpreter. This allowed me to learn from the opportunities and limits of association-based training while strengthening my interest in the theoretical foundations of the profession.

ITM

Formal academic training

I studied several semesters in the LSC–Spanish Interpretation program at ITM. I eventually had to leave because I was simultaneously studying Systems and Computer Engineering and working in the mornings. The program required a schedule that I could not realistically maintain.

Today

Interpreting, translating and continuing to grow

I currently work at Universidad Tecnológica de Pereira. This experience has shown me that I can exercise my profession with vocation while continuing to learn, meet new people and challenge my curiosity. I am also completing a technologist program in LSC–Spanish Interpretation and Translation, with the goal of continuing my academic path afterwards.

What connects all these stages?
The conviction that professional growth is not a finished destination. For me, interpreting is a way of combining language, service, learning, human connection and curiosity.
Looking ahead

Two professions, one direction.

My background in Systems and Computer Engineering gives me another way to understand problems and create solutions. My work in interpreting and translation gives those solutions a human and communicative dimension. In the future, I would like to bring both fields together through meaningful projects that use technology, language and accessibility to solve real problems.
